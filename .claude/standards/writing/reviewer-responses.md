# Writing voice: responses to reviewers

Apply `@~/.claude/standards/writing/CORE.md`. Tone shift: agreement-first and
deferential, even when the reviewer is wrong. Quotes from his own-hand 2025
response letters.

## Default move: concede the principle, then pivot to the evidence
> "We thank the reviewer for sharpening this point. We agree that correlation alone — even on a large dataset — does not constitute a predictive demonstration." (dms-3d-features/response_letter_redraft_4_28_26.md)

(The source uses em-dashes; in new prose render this as commas or parentheses per the CORE no-dash override: "correlation alone, even on a large dataset, does not constitute...")

Then point to the new analysis or figure that settles it; mark the turn with "Critically":
> "Critically, the constraint does not specify which base-pair class to adopt." (same)

## Rules
- Open each reply by thanking the reviewer for the specific point.
- When you disagree, assume the gap is in your explanation, not their reading. Soften, then cite the figure/data.
- **Never sound defensive or combative.** He explicitly removes "we already did this" phrasing and softens "what we believe was the core ambiguity" to "a conceptual distinction that may have been implicit." Let the evidence argue.
- Sign-off is the one place "I" appears: "Joseph D. Yesselman, on behalf of all authors."
- Keep CORE habits: active voice, articulated sentences, numbers inline, no em-dashes.
