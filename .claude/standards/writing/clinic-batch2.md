## 2A (results-open, dense, staccato)
> We probed 2,208 constructs with DMS at 20 °C. The signal-to-noise ratio exceeded 4 for 2,141 of them. We discarded the rest. Mutation fractions at unpaired adenines averaged 0.042. Paired adenines averaged 0.004. The ratio is about 10-fold. This gap narrows in junctions with three or more consecutive non-canonical pairs. There the ratio drops to 3.2-fold. Stacking explains part of this.

## 2B (results, dense, padded with connectives)
> To investigate the relationship between junction size and reactivity, we first calculated the mean mutation fraction for each junction class. Interestingly, 1×1 junctions showed a mean of 0.031, while 2×2 junctions showed 0.048, representing a 55% increase. Furthermore, 3×3 junctions continued this trend with a mean of 0.061. It is worth noting that this pattern held across both adenine and cytosine. Moreover, these findings suggest that larger junctions are more dynamic, which is an important consideration for structural modeling.

## 2E (results, one 90-word sentence; where is the upper bound?)
> Because the reactivity of a nucleotide reflects both its base-pairing state and its local solvent environment, and because these two contributions cannot be separated using a single reagent applied under a single condition, we performed paired probing with DMS and TMO across 713 junctions at two temperatures, which allowed us to decompose the measured mutation fraction into a pairing-dependent component that accounts for 61% of the variance and an environment-dependent component that accounts for the remaining 39%, a decomposition that would not be accessible from either reagent alone.
